1. Examination Method

3. Anatomical Landmarks

  • Point A: posterior border of the mandibular ramus.
  • Point B: gonion, representing the mandibular angle.
  • Point C: inferior border of the mandibular body.

The gonial angle is calculated as ∠ABC.

4. Forensic Significance

The gonial angle is a morphometric characteristic of mandibular architecture that has been investigated in forensic anthropology and forensic odontological research.

Its potential forensic applications include:

  • Age assessment: Changes in mandibular morphology during adult life have been investigated in relation to chronological age. The gonial angle may therefore provide supplementary information when used with established dental and skeletal age indicators.
  • Sex assessment: Gonial angle has been investigated as one of several mandibular measurements that may contribute to biological sex estimation.
  • Human identification: Mandibular morphology may contribute to comparative assessment when antemortem and postmortem radiographs are available.
  • Forensic anthropology: The angle can be incorporated into multivariate mandibular morphometric analysis.
Important: Gonial angle should not be used as a standalone determinant of age, sex or identity. Its forensic value is greatest when combined with other validated dental, skeletal and anthropological parameters.

7. Statistical and Forensic Limitations

  • Gonial angle varies between individuals.
  • Age, sex, population, dentition and mandibular morphology may influence the measurement.
  • Patient positioning and panoramic image distortion can influence the apparent angle.
  • Different observers may select slightly different anatomical landmarks.
  • The present application performs geometric calculation from examiner-selected landmarks. It does not automatically identify gonion.
  • The sex probability model is population- and modality-specific.
  • The age estimate has substantially greater uncertainty than the precision displayed by the numerical angle.

8. Forensic Conclusion

The forensic conclusion will be generated after measurement.

9. Examiner Statement

The reported gonial-angle measurements are based on landmarks selected by the examiner on the supplied panoramic radiograph. The software performs geometric calculation and statistical reference analysis but does not independently establish the anatomical identity of the selected landmarks. The findings should therefore be interpreted within the complete clinical, radiological and forensic context of the individual case.

10. Mathematical Method

The angle is calculated from vectors BA and BC:

θ = cos⁻¹[(BA · BC) / (|BA| × |BC|)]

The result is expressed in degrees.
